# Plan methodology (opinionated defaults)

Sensible defaults for building a runner's plan. These are starting heuristics, not laws — adapt to the individual, and prefer their stated preferences and history over any rule here. Use the athlete's units throughout.

## Choosing the approach
- **Specific race with a date** → periodized build that peaks ~1–3 weeks before race day, working backward from the goal.
- **Distance milestone** (first 5K/10K/half/marathon) → gradual base + distance progression, race-specific work late.
- **Consistency / general fitness** → a repeatable weekly template with gentle progression and no hard deadline.
Anchor the starting volume to the athlete's recent weekly total (from activity-log), not an ideal.

## Phases (for goal races)
Base → Build → Peak → Taper. Rough split of available weeks: Base ~40%, Build ~35%, Peak ~15%, Taper ~10%. Short timelines compress the early phases first.

## Volume progression
- Increase weekly volume gradually — on the order of ~5–10% per week for most runners; less when volume is already high or history is injury-prone.
- Every ~3–4 weeks, insert a **down week** (cut ~20–30%) to absorb the work.
- Avoid large single jumps; a missed block means rebuilding, not resuming at peak.

## Hard/easy balance
- Roughly **80% easy / 20% hard** by time/volume. Easy means conversational.
- Most runners: **2–3 quality sessions per week max**, never on back-to-back days. Recreational/beginner: often 1.
- Surround hard days and the long run with easy or rest days.

## The long run
- Typically ~20–30% of weekly volume; progress its length gradually and give it a down week too.
- Cap it to the goal: e.g. marathon long runs build toward ~16–22 mi (26–35 km) depending on the runner; don't chase distance for its own sake.

## Session types
Easy, Long, Tempo/Threshold, Intervals/VO2, Strides, Recovery, Rest, and (optional) cross-training/strength. Choose the quality sessions that fit the goal — e.g. threshold + long for the half/marathon; intervals + threshold for 5K/10K.

## Taper
For goal races, reduce volume over the final ~1–3 weeks (longer for longer races) while keeping some intensity so fitness stays sharp. Hand detailed race-week pacing/checklists to `/race-week`.

## Adapting to injuries & constraints
- Honor every **Training impact** limit in `health-log.md` (e.g. "no speedwork", "cap long run"). When in doubt, reduce load and suggest `/physical-therapist`.
- Fit quality and the long run to the athlete's **available days**; put the long run on their preferred day.
- If time is tight, protect easy volume and the long run before adding intensity.
