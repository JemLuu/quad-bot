# Fueling principles (a sports-nutrition palette)

Opinionated defaults for fueling a runner — pick what fits the question and adapt to the athlete. Everything here is **performance-fueling**: fuel the work and eat enough. Use the athlete's units.

## Red-flag screen (check first)
Signs that call for a **registered dietitian or doctor** — refer out, don't coach:
- Persistent fatigue, frequent illness or injury, stalled/declining performance despite training.
- Loss of menstrual cycle, always feeling cold, poor recovery, recurring stress fractures (possible **RED-S / low energy availability**).
- Preoccupation with food or weight, restriction, guilt around eating, or any disordered-eating pattern.
- A medical condition needing clinical nutrition (diabetes, GI disease, kidney issues, etc.).

## Workout fueling
- **Before:** carb-focused, lower fat/fiber to avoid GI trouble; more food the more time you have (a fuller meal ~2–3 h prior, a small snack ~30–60 min prior). Short easy runs need little or nothing.
- **During:** only for efforts beyond ~60–90 min — aim ~30–60 g carb/hour (up to ~90 with glucose:fructose mixes for long runs/races), plus fluids and sodium to match sweat.
- **After:** within the recovery window (~30–60 min) when training hard or twice a day — carbs + ~20–30 g protein.

## Daily framework
- **Athlete's plate**, scaled to the day: easy/rest day → smaller carb portion, more veg; hard/long day → bigger carb portion. Protein and veg at most meals; fats moderate.
- **Hand portions** (no weighing): palm = protein, cupped hand(s) = carbs, fist = veg, thumb = fats. Scale the carb portion up on hard days.
- Spread protein across ~4 eating occasions.

## Optional gram targets (only if body weight is known)
- Carbs ~3–5 g/kg/day (easy / low volume) up to ~8–10 g/kg/day (high volume / race build).
- Protein ~1.6–2.0 g/kg/day.
Give ranges; don't turn it into calorie counting.

## Hydration & micronutrients
- Hydration rule of thumb: pale-yellow urine; drink to thirst plus extra for long/hot runs; add sodium if a salty or heavy sweater.
- **Iron:** runners (especially female, vegetarian, or vegan) are prone to low iron — if persistently fatigued, suggest getting bloodwork; don't blindly supplement.

## Example meals
Offer **2–3 riffable options**, never a fixed plan. Respect restrictions/preferences, budget, and prep time. Frame as "something like…" so the athlete adapts to taste and what's in the kitchen.

## Race fueling
- **Carb-load** the day or two before longer races (raise carbs, ease off fiber/fat).
- **Race morning:** a familiar, mostly-carb meal ~2–4 h before — nothing new on race day.
- **In-race:** a rehearsed carb + fluid + sodium plan at regular intervals.
- **Gut training:** practice race fueling during long runs so the gut tolerates it.
- Defer taper, logistics, and the race-day checklist to `/race-week`.
