# Triage protocol

A structured way to think through a runner's complaint like a PT would — without ever diagnosing. Move through the stages in order. Ask follow-ups **one cluster at a time**, not all at once.

## Contents
- Stage 0: Red-flag screen (do this first)
- Stage 1: History clusters
- Stage 2: Hedged assessment
- Stage 3: Recommendations
- Common runner patterns (reference, not labels)
- When to refer

## Stage 0 — Red-flag screen (first, always)
If any of these are present, **don't try to "treat" it** — recommend prompt evaluation by a medical professional (and that they stop running on it):
- Focal, pinpoint **bone** pain; pain at rest or at night; pain when hopping on the leg — can suggest a stress fracture.
- Inability to bear weight, severe swelling, visible deformity, or a joint that locks/gives way.
- **Numbness, tingling, or weakness**, or pain radiating down a limb — possible nerve involvement.
- Calf pain/swelling/warmth (especially after travel/immobility) — needs to be ruled out medically.
- Fever, redness, heat over a joint, or feeling systemically unwell — possible infection.
- Chest pain, breathlessness, or dizziness with exertion — stop and seek urgent/emergency care.
- Pain steadily **worsening despite rest**, or that followed a significant trauma.

## Stage 1 — History clusters
Ask in small groups, adapting to answers:
1. **Location & nature:** where exactly? pain quality (sharp, dull, achy, burning)? can you point to one spot or is it diffuse?
2. **Timeline:** when did it start? sudden or gradual? better or worse since?
3. **Behavior:** what makes it worse / better? worse at the start of a run, during, or after? next-day stiffness?
4. **Mechanical/swelling:** any swelling, clicking, locking, giving way, or stiffness (e.g. worst in the morning)?
5. **Load & context:** recent changes in mileage, intensity, hills, surfaces, or **shoes**? new to running or returning? (cross-check `activity-log.md` for load spikes.)
6. **History:** had this (or related issues) before? what helped or didn't?

## Stage 2 — Hedged assessment
- Offer possibilities, never a verdict. Use language like *"this pattern is **consistent with**…"*, *"often this kind of thing **points toward**…"*, *"a PT would likely **want to rule out**…"*. **Never** say "you have X."
- Offer the 1–2 most likely patterns and what would distinguish them, and note what you're *not* sure about.

## Stage 3 — Recommendations
Frame as "what often helps" and "what a PT might suggest":
- **Load management** (usually relative rest, not total rest): reduce or modify the aggravating activity; cross-train to keep fitness if pain allows.
- **Symptom care:** sensible short-term measures appropriate to the issue.
- **Strengthening / mobility:** the progressive work that typically addresses the contributing weakness (this is the real fix for most overuse issues). Record it as a standing routine.
- **Address contributors:** load spikes, footwear age/fit, running form factors — gently.
- **Graded return:** simple rules for progressing (e.g. only add load when symptoms stay mild and settle quickly), and what would mean "back off."

## Common runner patterns (reference — use as possibilities, not labels)
- **Patellofemoral ("runner's knee"):** diffuse front-of-knee pain; worse with stairs, downhill, prolonged sitting. Often hip/quad strength + load.
- **IT band syndrome:** lateral (outer) knee pain, often at a consistent point in the run, worse downhill.
- **Achilles tendinopathy:** pain/stiffness in the tendon, worst in the morning and at the start of activity. Responds to progressive calf loading.
- **Plantar fasciitis:** underside-of-heel pain, classic first-steps-in-the-morning pain.
- **Medial tibial stress syndrome (shin splints):** diffuse pain along the inner shin (note: *focal* shin bone pain is a stress-fracture red flag).
- **Patellar tendinopathy:** pain just below the kneecap.
- **Calf/hamstring strain:** more sudden onset during effort; graded loading return.
General principle for overuse/tendon issues: **manage load + progressively strengthen**; rest alone usually isn't the fix.

## When to refer
Recommend seeing a professional if: red flags appear, it's not improving over ~1–2 weeks of sensible management, it's worsening, it keeps recurring, or the runner is anxious and would benefit from hands-on assessment. Framing: a PT can examine in person and tailor things in ways this conversation can't.
